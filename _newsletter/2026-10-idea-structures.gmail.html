Group, Synthesize and Rise above: three ways to grow bigger ideas from the cards your class already has.
✧ wonder:bits View in browser

Hi there,

Your students may already be building knowledge together: writing cards, building on each other’s ideas, and adding hashtags to ideas.

As a canvas grows, those ideas can start to form something larger. A new issue is to bring "clutter" to useful structures to build new, more powerful ideas.

Below are a few things you can try with your class.

1. From cards to bigger ideas

WonderBits has three ways to turn many cards into something larger. Each answers a different question:

  • Group — which of these are about the same thing? Gather related cards under a name, so a topic can be seen at a glance.
  • Synthesize — what do these add up to? Write a new Synthesis card over several ideas, saying what they add up to that none of them says alone.
  • Rise above (teachers only) — what has the class settled? Lift the ideas everything else now builds on into a new level: a fresh starting point for the next stretch of work.

Group and Synthesize start with selecting multiple cards. Show your class:

  • Shift-click each card. Shift-click a card again to take it back out. (⌘-click on a Mac and Ctrl-click on Windows work too.)
  • Or hold Shift and drag a box across canvas. This method works when cards are already near each other.

Select two or more cards, and Group and Synthesize appear.

Group asks for a name, and when you save, the cards will be put under it. The group can be then collapsed, with details hidden from canvas.

Synthesize opens a writing space with the chosen ideas beside it; the student needs to synthesize these ideas into a bigger one.

A synthesis, written with the ideas it draws on in view.

Try this at the end of a lesson: ask pairs to find three or four cards that belong together and decide — group or synthesize? Then have two or three pairs explain their choice to the class.

Rise above is your move for the end of a unit or a long discussion. Zoom out, and once the canvas has enough ideas, a Rise above button appears at the bottom. Click on it, it shows you which ideas would rise. Click to confirm. Nothing is deleted: the original ideas stays one level below, and anyone can step back down to it. You can also manually pull up an idea to the new level, or push down an idea to the original level. Consider do this with your class, and make sure students understand the purpose and where they can find their original ideas if needed.

2. Pages: more room in the same space

Pages give a topic, a small group, or a student portfolio its own canvas inside the same space. Open the page menu next to the space name to switch pages, or choose Add new page.

To move an idea to another page, right-click the card and choose Share / Copy. Under This space, each page offers:

  • Move — the card relocates. Students can move their own cards.
  • Copy — an independent duplicate that lives its own life there.
  • Share — the same card appears on both pages, and edits show in both. Only the card’s author can share it.

It works for a selection too: Share / copy is also in the panel you get after selecting several cards. Pages walkthrough →

Even better, you as teacher can change the default landing page for your class space, so that your students land directly on where they should be.
