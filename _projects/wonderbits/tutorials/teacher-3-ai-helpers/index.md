Every AI helper is off in a new space, and that is deliberate — AI in a classroom should be a decision you make, not a default you inherit. This clip covers what a helper sees, what to settle before you switch one on, and then introduces the three your students would meet.

## In this clip

- Nothing a helper sees is retained after processing, and none of it trains AI models
- A space with AI switched off sends nothing at all
- Deciding what a helper is *for* before enabling it — and telling your class what you turned on, and why
- Why nothing lands on the canvas by itself: a suggestion waits to be accepted or dismissed
- **Pip** to talk to, **Buzz** for the shape of the idea landscape, **Shimmer** for connections nobody has drawn
- Where they live once they're on: the hive, beside the plus button

> **Try it:** Turn one on in a space of your own and use it the way a student would, until you can say what it is good at and where it goes wrong. That answer is what you owe your class before it meets them.
