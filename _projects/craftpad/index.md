## Overview

CraftPad is a lesson design workspace built around two things that make teaching a profession: **pedagogical reasoning** and **professional judgment**. Teachers work with AI in CraftPad, but the design decisions, and the reasons behind them, stay with the teacher.

Most AI tools for teachers promise efficiency: produce a lesson plan faster, fill in a template, move on. That skips the part that matters most. A good lesson design comes from reasoning about learning goals, about students, and about why one activity should come before another, and from judging what will work in a particular classroom. CraftPad makes that reasoning visible and keeps it in the teacher's hands, instead of hiding it inside a generated plan.

## Approach

**Foregrounding pedagogical reasoning.** CraftPad asks teachers to articulate and examine the reasoning behind a design: what students should learn, how each activity supports that, and which pedagogical principles are at work. AI is used to prompt and extend that reasoning, not to replace it.

**Sustaining professional judgment.** Teachers decide what to keep, adapt, or reject. AI-generated material arrives as a proposal to evaluate, never as a finished answer, so the final design reflects the teacher's judgment about their own students and context.

**Teachers as designers, not end-users.** Even the most advanced AI lesson planners tend to work inside rigid templates that leave teachers little room to exercise their expertise. We take a **meta-design** approach, which means designing for design itself: teachers can configure and adapt the tool to serve their own pedagogical goals.

## What CraftPad Offers

- **An infinite canvas.** An open, Figma-like space where teachers lay out, connect, and rework design ideas, so the structure of their reasoning stays visible.
- **A pedagogical prompt library.** Prompts grounded in pedagogical principles that guide AI generation for specific design tasks.
- **A flexible workflow.** Teachers can start a design quickly, refine individual components with their own input, and compile complete design artifacts (such as lesson designs) in which AI-generated content passes through their judgment.
- **A reflective AI coach.** A coach that can see the design canvas and knows the relevant pedagogical principles. It asks teachers questions about their design choices rather than simply producing output.
